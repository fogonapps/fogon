Privacy policy

Fogón Social and its apps (El Cajón, El Forzudo, La Cucha and La Alcancía) · In force since 13 November 2026 (version 2026-11-13) · Español

This is a translation. If it differs from the Spanish version, the Spanish version applies.

In short

Fogón apps work without an account, and that way nothing of yours reaches our server. One Fogón Social account works for all of them; if you create one, only what you decide to share from each app reaches us (and an encrypted copy of each app’s data that only your password opens). We don’t sell data, show ads or track you. To search for films, books or music, the app queries public catalogues (section 3).

1. Who is responsible

Angel, on behalf of Fogón, in Colombia. Email: fogonapps@gmail.com. Requests about your data are handled at that address.

2. What data we process and why

  • Your account: username, the name you display and your email. They identify you, let other people find you, let us send you sign-in codes and security notices (for example, if your password or email changes), and let you sign in from another phone. We also keep an internal account identifier that doesn’t change if you change your username.
  • What you share: your photo, cover, bio, links, the profile sections you mark as public or for your followers, your posts (reviews, diary entries, lists, photos —up to 10 per post— or a video of up to 30 seconds, which the app prepares on your phone before uploading it) your 24-hour note and stories (a photo or a video of up to 15 seconds, a text and, if you want, something attached: a song, a work, a workout, a dish or a goal as a percentage). Your profile store items: the background and frame you wear (seen by everyone who visits your profile), what you bought and your sparks, with each movement (welcome, daily, purchases): the one you wear is seen by everyone who visits your profile. If you want, your birthday (day and month only): only your friends (people who follow you back) see it, and it is used to remind them that day. For each story we keep which of your people saw it, and only you see that. When a story expires it goes to your archive, which only you can see, and is deleted after 90 days (the ones you highlight on your profile stay until you remove them); if one is removed by moderation, it is kept hidden for its period. In El Forzudo, the workouts you decide to share: title, date, duration and the exercises and sets you choose, never your body weight or measurements. In La Cucha, the dishes, recipes and restaurant reviews you decide to share; calories and macros only if you include them in that post. In La Alcancía, the achievements you decide to share (that you reached a goal, a goal’s progress as a percentage, your first entry or the months you closed) and, if you choose, your goals with their progress as a percentage; never balances, amounts, accounts, banks or transactions. They are shown to whoever you chose, in the app you posted them from.
  • Places: if you review a restaurant, its name, city and, if you add them, the address and location (with “Use my location” the phone gives its position only when you tap it; we store it rounded, to about 11 metres). Anyone who can see the review can see the place; the exact location is not shown on the web. We keep who created each place, for moderation.
  • The app of origin: which app you sign in, post or request a code from (El Cajón, El Forzudo, La Cucha or La Alcancía), to show each thing in its app and name it in emails.
  • What you do on the network: who you follow, who follows you, your likes (on posts and comments), your “Helpful” votes on reviews, your vote for a work’s cover or image (only totals are shown), your comments and replies, saved posts, blocks and reports. They build your feed, your notifications and the counts, and keep the network safe.
  • Views: to count how many times a post was seen we keep a fingerprint that doesn’t reveal who saw it, and we delete it after 14 days.
  • Messages: they are end-to-end encrypted. The server only keeps the encrypted message (it can’t read it) until it is delivered to each app where you use messages, and for 30 days at most; your private chat keys are stored encrypted with your password (the server can’t open them), so you can use messages in all four apps and on another phone; conversations are stored only on your phone. Photos and voice notes you send travel inside the encrypted message; the microphone is used only while you record a voice note, with your permission. “Seen” receipts are encrypted too and you can turn them off. It also keeps your phone’s public keys, so others can write to you.
  • Your sessions: the make and model of each phone you signed in on, the app and the dates, so you can see “Where you’re signed in” and close the ones you don’t recognise. We only keep a fingerprint of the session. Unused sessions are deleted after 90 days.
  • Sign-in security: failed password attempts (deleted after a day), codes sent to each email (deleted after an hour) and a pending email change. Your IP address is used only in memory to stop abuse and is not stored.
  • Password, if you create one: only its fingerprint (PBKDF2), never the password.
  • Your apps’ data saved to your account: with an account, your drawer, your El Forzudo notebook, your La Cucha diary and your La Alcancía notebook are saved automatically, so they come back if you reinstall or change phones. They are encrypted on your phone with a key that only your password opens. We keep the encrypted envelopes and can’t read them; if you forget your password, we can’t recover them (what’s on your phone stays). They are deleted when you delete your account.
  • Proof that you accepted this policy (version and date), as the law requires.
  • Reports and moderation: who reported, what, why and what we decided, and each account’s strikes. They keep the network safe and help us comply with the law. Resolved reports, decisions and expired strikes are deleted after a year. Content removed by moderation is kept hidden for 90 days in case it needs review and then deleted. Content that puts a minor at risk (for example, child sexual abuse) is removed, reported to the authorities and kept hidden for one year, accessible only to the team that decides on it, to hand it to them; then it is deleted, unless an authority asks us to keep it longer.
  • Notifications on your phone: if you have an account and notifications on, we keep a notification identifier for each app on your phone (issued by Firebase Cloud Messaging, from Google). It is used to wake the app when you have something new; it is deleted when you turn notifications off, sign out or it stops working.
  • The moderation team: if you are part of it, we keep your role, your permissions, your second factor (encrypted) and a log of every team action. That log is never deleted: it protects people from abuse by the team itself.
  • Shared lists (El Cajón): if you make a list with friends or someone adds you to one, we keep its name, who is in it, the works each person adds (title, type, cover and where it came from) and who voted for each one. Only the people in the list see it. If you leave, you stop seeing it; if the person who made it leaves, it is deleted for everyone.
  • Greeting phrases: if you use your account to like a phrase on the home screen, share it or ask not to see it again, we keep that vote. Only totals are shown, never who voted. If you suggest a phrase, we keep its text and that it was yours (only the team sees this); if it’s approved, it appears without your name.
  • Why you leave: if you choose a reason when deleting your account, we keep it without anything that identifies you.

What is private in your drawer (your private diary, private reviews, notes, what you listen to, your tastes and what the app learns from them), your El Forzudo notebook (unshared workouts, body weight, measurements and routines) your La Cucha diary (what you eat each day, your goals, your weight and your personal details) and your La Alcancía notebook (accounts, balances, transactions, envelopes, your goals’ amounts and what it reads from bank notifications) are never sent to the server in a readable form: they only travel inside that encrypted copy, which nobody else can open. We don’t ask for sensitive data and ask you not to post it.

3. Services the apps query from your phone

To search for and complete what you save, El Cajón queries public catalogues. These services see your IP address and what is searched, like any website, but not your account or your drawer:

  • Songs for notes and stories: the search goes to Apple (iTunes) through our server, so Apple doesn’t see your IP or your search; the 30-second preview plays from Apple’s servers (they do see your IP when you listen) and the “Listen on Apple Music” button opens its page. A film or series trailer opens on YouTube.
  • Films, series and anime: TMDB, through our server (TMDB doesn’t see your IP; the server doesn’t keep your searches, only the answers for a few hours to avoid repeating requests), its images at image.tmdb.org, and TVmaze.
  • Books: Open Library and its covers.
  • Music: MusicBrainz, ListenBrainz, Cover Art Archive and, only if you connect it with your own Client ID, your Spotify account (read-only).
  • Photos and data about people and works: Wikidata, Wikipedia and Wikimedia Commons.
  • Your Last.fm history, only if you import it: you type your Last.fm username and the app requests your public scrobbles through our server (Last.fm doesn’t see your IP). The server doesn’t keep your username or what you listen to: it only holds the answer in memory for a few minutes to avoid repeating requests. What you import stays on your phone.

A place’s address (La Cucha): if you tap “Use my location”, your phone’s address service turns the position into an address you can correct. Depending on the phone, that service is Google’s; if your phone doesn’t have one, you type the address yourself.

What you listen to: if you grant notification access, the app records what plays in your music apps. That stays on your phone and only leaves it if you put it on your profile.

Links to IMDb, Letterboxd, Goodreads, Spotify, Apple Music or Deezer open in your browser, outside the app.

4. Who we share it with

No one, for commercial purposes. What you mark as public can be seen by anyone, also on the web (for example, at your profile link); what you mark for your followers, only by them. The server is hosted by Vultr in Miami (United States), a country included by Colombia’s Superintendence of Industry and Commerce in its list of countries with an adequate level of protection; Vultr only provides the machine and doesn’t use the data. Emails are sent with Gmail (Google). If you have notifications on, when you have something new we send your phone a signal through Firebase Cloud Messaging (Google): it is empty, with no text and no sender, and the app asks our server for the rest; Google only knows there was a signal for that phone. On phones without Google services, the app checks on its own from time to time. We only hand data to an authority with a valid order or when the law requires us to report.

5. Your rights

As the data subject you can know, update, correct and delete your data, revoke your authorisation, request proof of it, learn how it is used and complain to the Superintendence of Industry and Commerce (Colombian Law 1581 of 2012, art. 8).

  • From the app: edit your profile whenever you want, make anything private (it is removed from the server), delete your posts and close your sessions. In Settings → Account you can delete your account: it stops being visible immediately, your other sessions are closed and you have 7 days to change your mind; after that, everything of yours is deleted from the server.
  • By email: write to fogonapps@gmail.com from your account’s email or stating your username. Queries are answered within 10 business days and complaints within 15 business days (Law 1581, arts. 14 and 15).

6. How long

While you have the account, with the shorter periods listed in section 2. When you delete it (7 days after you ask), its data is removed from the server, except what moderation or the law requires us to keep as explained above. Reports you made are kept without your data. Once a day an encrypted copy of the server is made on a computer belonging to the controller, in Colombia, to restore the service if something fails: we keep the last 30 days, one per week for 12 weeks and one per month for 12 months. So what you delete may remain in those copies for up to a year, unused for anything else, and then disappears.

7. Minors

Fogón Social is only for people aged 18 or over. If we learn that an account belongs to a minor, we close it.

8. Security

Everything travels encrypted (HTTPS). Your session is stored encrypted on the phone and only its fingerprint stays on the server. Email sign-in uses single-use codes that expire in 10 minutes, and the moderation team signs in with a second factor.

9. Changes

If this policy changes, the app shows it to you and asks you to accept it again before you keep using the network. If you don’t agree, you can delete your account.
